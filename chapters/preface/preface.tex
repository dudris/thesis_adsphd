\section*{Preface}
My PhD journey commenced in May 2018, marked by enthusiasm and a deep curiosity for delving into an impactful and dynamic field of research. Joining one of Europe’s most esteemed universities, I was inspired by the opportunity to advance my understanding of computational materials science. My initial excitement was rooted in the foundation I built during my previous research experience at the Institute of Plasma Physics, Czech Academy of Sciences, Prague, where I had the honor of defending an award-winning thesis. This accomplishment strengthened my resolve to tackle new scientific challenges with ambition and purpose.

Over the course of my doctoral studies, I have grown both academically and personally, gaining resilience and learning to adapt to the evolving demands of a PhD. While the journey presented moments of difficulty—particularly during the pandemic years—the experience has ultimately reinforced my commitment to scientific rigor and perseverance. The PhD environment in Leuven has fostered my development as a researcher and has shaped my approach to problem-solving, both independently and collaboratively.

I am especially grateful to my supervisor, Prof. Nele Moelans, whose guidance and expertise have been invaluable to my progress. Her insightful feedback on my research, publications, and milestones has been crucial in shaping the direction of my work. It has been a privilege to contribute to the INTERDIFFUSION ERC project, whose funding provided a stable foundation for my research. Prof. Moelans’ ability to rapidly navigate complex scientific challenges and identify key insights has set a standard that I aspire to follow in my own career.

I would like to extend my sincere gratitude to the members of my Examination Committee: Prof. Dr. Ir. Lucie Vandewalle, Prof. Dr. Marc Seefeldt, Prof. Dr. Ir. Kim Vanmeensel, Prof. Dr. Ir. Joris Proost from UCLouvain, DSc. Tamás Pusztai, Ph.D. from Wigner Research Centre for Physics, and Dr.-Ing. Daniel Schneider from Karlsruhe Institute of Technology. I am deeply appreciative of their time, effort, and valuable insights in reviewing this dissertation.

Many aspects of my PhD life I enjoyed dearly. The intellectual freedom, the privilege to immerse into the state-of-the art science, all the discussions and learning. The first big conference without any stress. Small moments in the beautiful botanical garden or the inspiring ambiance of the amazing libraries and study rooms, where one can feel the serious presence of the many books filled with knowledge. New perspectives, friendships, food, and search for what feels good in my life. I am proud to be a KU Leuven alumnus and certainly much richer thanks to the experience.

I was lucky to have great colleagues, who became important part of my academic growth: Vincent, Yuri, Xiaojing, Sourav and Vishal, with all of whom I led the most inspiring hours-long conversations about thermodynamics, phase field or just about life. The Friday evenings at the Chinese or Indian restaurant are the memories of togetherness, friendship and fun. Especially in the first year we were such a great study group, all so much into phase field and most of us in the first half of our PhDs. Xiaojing and her husband Zilong became so dear friends of mine and I cannot wait to meet their son.

I do not believe I would be able to carry my project on in the most hopeless times without Vincent Feyen. Our weekly meetings became a solid support I could lean on, a source of regular informal feedback, encouragement and an opportunity to provide the same in return. I have always admired your intellectual courage, orientation on results and an uncompromising focus on true value in research. Thank you so much, Vincent. I wish you, Lize and Isolde all the best life can bring you. 

I am grateful for support of other wonderful people I met at MTM: Siggi Wodarz and Wouter Monnens, who helped me with the few electrodeposition experiments I did; Alina Arslanova and Vadim Trepalin, who knew it all about the struggles I was going through; Ruben Buch, with whom it was so enjoyable to prepare for the practica and who became my friend the moment we met; Yijie Liang who has always been so kind and positive. Another beautiful friendships I found among the Iranian colleagues: Ali, Babak, Ensieh, who eventually could reunite with her husband Ali and I was so happy for you.

Also, I want to express my gratitude to my Master thesis supervisor and friend from IPP František Lukáč, who gave me moral support and general advice several times. Similarly, I am grateful to prof. Michal Beneš from my alma mater, who is an expert on phase field method himself and despite his many obligations he proofread some of my mathematical derivations in functional calculus. Knowing that I had carried them out properly allowed me to focus on the next steps with more confidence.

Especially after the fourth year of my PhD, when I got no funding and started part-time working in the automotive industry, I could strongly recognize the support from my family. With this, I deeply appreciate the support from my mum, Eva Dudrová, which enabled the part-time job and part-time PhD scheme, providing the relevant two days weekly for my PhD and sufficient funds for my family. This thesis was mostly written during four separate weeks of vacation I spent in Chotusice, a small village in Czechia, where my father-in-law Josef Sládek made sure I had nothing to worry about except for the thesis. I loved our lunches and talks about life. Without these "retreats", my progress would have been even harder and I am very grateful for this support as well. 

The same goes for Thomas Lee, who helped me getting up when one of the more difficult moments crossed my path and has always been so supportive and understanding.

I also want to appreciate the lively, supportive and friendly environment created by all the MTM'ers I have not mentioned explicitly, as well as all my friends and family members who were always tuned in my ups and downs. 

The biggest thanks of all goes to my wife Alena Minar, with whom I accepted a common new surname after we got married because it made sense. She did everything humanly possible to support me in getting here, was always by my side, and willing to accommodate the needs of the PhD into our life, uncompromising on the ambition to which we both sacrificed so much. I took a lot of the perseverance and toughness necessary to finish this from her lead. Accomplishing the PhD means a closure on many levels for us both, and I hope that also more of the cheerful colors of life in the coming years. 

\section*{Use of generative AI in this thesis}
The first complete draft of the thesis was written without any AI-generated text, but ChatGPT4 was used for brainstorming about goals of the thesis. In the following drafts, ChatGPT4 was used for improving the flow, grammar and overall comprehensibility in several places. This applies especially to the Preface, Abstract and General Conclusion chapters. The section Goals of the thesis was rewritten by ChatGPT4 from a bullet-point list to a fluent text, but the content remained essentially equal (i.e. no ideas were added by the AI). ChatGPT was also used to aid debugging of problems in LaTeX source compilation.

The whole manuscript was reviewed by the author and he takes full responsibility.
